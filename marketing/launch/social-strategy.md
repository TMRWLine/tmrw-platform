# TMRWLine | Social media strategy

## What we stand for

TMRWLine helps local businesses in Australia and Aotearoa New Zealand back athletes and grassroots clubs. Start with the people who keep local sport going. Show what it takes to get to game day and where the sponsorship money goes.

**Campaign idea:** Every postcode has a tomorrow. **Tagline:** Line up your Future. Use `TMRWLine` in prose and the lowercase `tmrw/.` wordmark in artwork.

Sound like someone who knows the ground, not a brand speaking from the stands. Write short, confident lines in NZ English. Be specific about time, cost and place when verified and safe to share. Never turn financial pressure into a sob story or promise a sponsor, a payout or a path to the pros.

## What to learn from the benchmarks

Use these accounts as references for craft, not as sources of footage, graphics, music, captions, logos or jerseys. Before briefing a new format, review recent posts from the relevant reference and note one framing, editing, writing or reply technique to try with original material. Never tag the benchmark accounts.

| Reference | What to learn | What TMRWLine makes | What stays theirs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Give people a reason to send it to a mate. | **Postcode Play:** an original moment from local sport, told through a tight setup, the play and the reaction. Ask who keeps their own game going. | Their highlight clips, punchlines and staged imitation of crowd reactions. |

## What we publish

Use this mix across a four-week run, not as a quota for each account. Count each published post once, under its main purpose. A reply is not a post. If nobody is cleared to appear, use anonymous ground details, unbranded dramatisations or worked examples. The first week is athlete-free.

| Pillar and share | Instagram | TikTok | LinkedIn |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Local sport in one frame: 45%** | **Postcode Play** Reels or **Pick the Moment** Stories: open on a striking action or ground detail, then ask for a share or vote. | **Before the Whistle**: open on natural sound and cut to the first moment of play. | **The Regulars** photo post: one club-approved volunteer story and a practical takeaway for local businesses. |
| **The work before matchday: 35%** | **The Work Before** carousel: three to five stills that follow the work before a game. | **Clock In. Run On.**: cut between a shift and matchday. Use a verified, cleared account or label the whole sequence a dramatisation with an unnamed actor. | **What It Takes** document: explain one verified cost or time commitment and what support could change. Use a labelled example when there is no cleared case. |
| **Follow the money: 20%** | **Where It Goes** carousel: one share per slide, with the total and status of the figures clear. | **Receipt in 20 Seconds**: read the total and all shares aloud with captions; answer one useful question in the next video. | **The Local Receipt** document: show the full split and invite a specific club or business reply. Label examples as samples, not payments, and use real figures only with evidence and permission. |

### The look and sound

- Use original black-and-white photography on carbon `#080808` and off-white `#F7F7F6`. Use one volt accent sparingly on dark. For the existing launch kit that is `#B8FF00`; the site direction proposes `#D2FF00` for a future site-wide palette. Do not mix greens or recolour the supplied social assets.
- Use heavy headlines of six words or fewer, with short, readable uppercase monospace captions for verified times, scores and receipt figures. Use straight edges and fine rules to organise information, never as part of the logo. Move type or transition the footage with a 23° cut; keep the mark still. Keep captions clear of app buttons, make covers read at phone size and avoid rapid flashes. The story must make sense on mute.
- Use the [dark primary lockup](assets/logo-dark.svg) on dark with the volt slash and dot, or the all-ink [light primary lockup](assets/logo-light.svg) on off-white. Both use bold `LINE UP YOUR FUTURE` directly below the mark, without a rule. Leave at least 0.4em of clear space. Use the [app icon](assets/app-icon.svg) in the [circular-crop avatar artwork](assets/profile-picture.png). Never retype or animate the logo. The supplied hairline and monospace variants are web references, not social marks. Take the monospace style into the numbers and time captions instead.

### Cadence and one-shoot workflow

After the [first-week plan](account-setup.md), start with two Instagram Reels and one carousel a week, plus Stories on two days; three TikToks a week, with one slot for a reply video when there is a useful question; and one LinkedIn document or still a week. Keep local match times in mind and use the first month to test posting times separately by platform. If a shoot is delayed, run a cleared still or sample instead of filling a slot with someone else's footage.

1. **Before the shoot:** choose a single story and action for each platform. Clear location, people, figures and rights first. Capture vertical action, clean stills with room for type and natural sound. For an athlete-free shoot, shoot tight on court lines, boots and floodlights without faces, landmarks, signage or identifying marks.
2. **From the same shoot:** make a short Instagram Reel with a strong cover and a reason to share, a TikTok edit that starts with the strongest sound or action and asks one question, and a silent LinkedIn document or still that connects the story to a practical sponsorship decision. For instance, use a painted line and a ball bounce for an Instagram introduction, a boot-lacing sequence for a labelled TikTok dramatisation, and a still from the setup for a worked LinkedIn receipt. Don't imply that the footage records a real sponsorship or athlete's shift.
3. **Publish from a clean master:** export without watermarks or library audio. Write the cover, caption, on-screen subtitles and action for each app. Add music separately in-app and log rights and approvals before scheduling.

## From zero to 500,000 real followers

These are targets, not forecasts or deadlines. Track followers and net change by account. If reporting a total, call it a sum of account counts, not unique people. Don't buy followers, trade follows or use giveaways that reward empty engagement. Advance only when the follower milestone **and** the readiness gate are met. The gates below are targets for operating readiness, not industry benchmarks.

| Phase | What we do | What we measure | Gate to advance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **0 to 1,000: earn the first follows** | Publish athlete-free originals, invite private postcode nominations, reply to every genuine question within one working day and start conversations with local clubs. Test two openings for the same series across different posts. | Net follows by app, full-watch rate by series, shares per 1,000 views, genuine replies, usable private nominations and reply time. | Reach 1,000 and run the weekly review for four weeks. Identify two repeatable formats that beat the account median for shares or full watches on at least two posts each. |
| **1,000 to 10,000: build repeat local interest** | Make posts with consenting clubs and local creators. Invite supporter submissions with a written reuse agreement; don't treat a tag as permission. Start athlete features only when cleared. Turn real questions into reply videos and follow up on nominations. | Returning viewers where available, shares and saves per 1,000 views, usable nominations, documented submissions and completed collaborations. | Reach 10,000, complete at least two cleared collaborations and see nominations from at least two verified postcodes for four consecutive weeks. |
| **10,000 to 100,000: repeat what works across postcodes** | Run cleared series in more regions in both countries. Collaborate with creators who already serve those communities. Set a small paid test budget for posts that already earn shares and meaningful replies, only where rights cover paid use. Compare each boost with an unboosted post in the same format. | Returning viewers where available, net follows, nominations and serious sponsor enquiries by region, plus cost per usable nomination and per serious enquiry for paid tests. | Reach 100,000, repeat one successful series in both countries for two publishing cycles and keep paid tests within agreed cost limits before increasing spend. |
| **100,000 to 500,000: protect trust at scale** | Give regional creators clear briefs, keep athlete-led stories and club submissions consented, and reinvest only in posts that bring useful local replies, nominations or enquiries. Reserve people and time for moderation and corrections. | Net follower growth by app, repeat viewing, shares, usable nominations and serious sponsor enquiries by region, paid cost per useful action, and rights or moderation issues. | At 500,000, keep the cadence only if four weeks of reviews show community replies within one working day, complete rights records and no unresolved rights issues. Otherwise hold spending and fix the gaps. |

### Community, rights and publishing

- Give each post one main action: follow for the next story, share with a mate or local business, answer a specific question, or nominate privately. Ask for the nominator's postcode first, not the athlete's name or contact details. Ask them to speak to the athlete before sharing any details. Don't solicit names in comments; hide identifying details and redirect to a private message. Move any junior nomination through a parent or guardian, not a direct conversation with the junior.
- Reply in our own voice. Acknowledge good-faith criticism, correct factual mistakes visibly, remove abuse or exposed personal information and don't turn a disagreement into content without permission. Don't reveal a private message, tagging trail or location through a screenshot.
- Before featuring anyone identifiable, record written consent and the right to use the specific photo, video and audio. Record who approved it, where it can run, for how long and whether edits, tags and paid use are covered. For juniors, verify permission from a parent or guardian before use. A signed licence flag alone does not clear a post. Never name examples, scouted prospects or people without permission; only use verified and cleared athlete postcodes. No team logos, club emblems or trademarked jerseys. When in doubt, hold the post or use an athlete-free alternative.
- Make or select original or rights-cleared pictures and footage. Use original ground sound first, or choose an instrumental from each app's in-app commercial-use library for Instagram and TikTok. Brief the mood, tempo and search terms, not a named track. Check whether the track can run in a paid post before boosting. Never carry the library track to another app. Use silent LinkedIn documents or original or LinkedIn-cleared audio for video.
- Use a few relevant hashtags, such as `#TMRWLine`, `#GrassrootsSport` and one topic tag such as `#SportsSponsorship`. No unrelated trending tags. Tag only athletes or clubs with documented permission for that post; otherwise write `None`. Leave location tags off anonymous shoots. Repost community footage only with written permission from the people shown and the rights holder. Get approval before publishing, tagging, reposting or boosting; the kit does not grant permission or send anything.

### Weekly review

Every Monday, record the previous week's posts by platform, series, opening and action. Compare rates within the same platform and format; use full watches as a share of video starts where reported, and use shares or saves per 1,000 views where counts are available. Separate paid from unpaid. Count a usable nomination once if it has an in-scope postcode and a permission-safe route to follow up; count a serious enquiry if a business identifies an area and asks for a next step. Keep only totals in the review, not private details.

Pick one thing to repeat, one change to test and one format to pause. Give each an owner and a date. Check the replies, what people actually asked for, the rights records and the current growth gate. Feed the decisions into the next daily campaign brief.
