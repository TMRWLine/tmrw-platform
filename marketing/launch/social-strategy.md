# TMRWLine | Social media strategy

## What we stand for

TMRWLine helps local businesses in Australia and Aotearoa New Zealand back athletes and grassroots clubs. Show the people who keep sport going, what it takes to get to game day and where the money goes.

**Campaign idea:** Every postcode has a tomorrow. **Tagline:** Line up your Future. Use `TMRWLine` in prose and the lowercase `tmrw/.` wordmark in artwork.

Write like someone who knows the ground. Use short, confident lines in NZ English. Be specific about cost, time and place only when verified and safe to share. Never turn financial pressure into a sob story or promise a sponsor, payout or path to the pros.

Before briefing a shoot, review recent posts from the relevant accounts and note one framing, editing, writing or reply technique to try. Use the references for craft only. Never copy their assets, sound, copy, logos, emblems or jerseys, or tag them.

## Three hero series

Start with this four-week mix across published posts. Start every episode with a local person, place or question that earns the campaign line. Close every end card with **Every postcode has a tomorrow**; keep the manual's bold `LINE UP YOUR FUTURE` under the wordmark. On text-only posts, use the campaign line as the close instead. Park **Pick the Moment**, **The Work Before** and **Clock In. Run On.** until a hero series earns repeat viewing or shares. Keep the old dramatisation only as an unscheduled fallback, not a fourth series.

| Series and share | Instagram | TikTok | LinkedIn |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Postcode People: 45%** | Reels about an adult player, coach or volunteer, plus the weekly Postcode Call-Out in Stories. | A recurring founder or consenting local host opens to camera, then shows the person or play; reply to useful comments with follow-up videos. | The founder tells one local person's cleared story or explains why nominations start with a postcode. |
| **Before the Whistle: 35%** | A Reel built around a real pre-game ritual, with a still from the action as the cover. | Open on a whistle, a ball or a first-person line; use a quick first cut and leave room for natural sound. | The founder uses a still or short video to explain one part of getting local sport onto the field. |
| **The Local Receipt: 20%** | A Reel or occasional carousel shows every share, including ours. | Show the total in the first frame, read every share aloud, and answer one question with the host. | A silent Company Page document shows the whole split, including fees, with a dated source for any cost claim. |

**Weekly ritual:** On Fridays, run the same **Postcode Call-Out** on TikTok and in Instagram Stories: “Who keeps your local game going? Send us your postcode privately first.” Start with the nominator's postcode only. Don't ask for names in comments. Report back the following week with a verified, cleared story or an anonymous theme; never quote private messages or publish identifying details without permission.

### Look, sound and covers

- Use monochrome action photography, carbon `#080808`, off-white `#F7F7F6` and a single volt, **`#B8FF00`**, as the shared brand choice for new work. This keeps the supplied identity art and matches the repo's palette. The site has legacy colours; migrating its tokens is separate from the launch kit. Use volt sparingly on dark only.
- Lead with a cleared still from the actual action. Put one bold headline of six words or fewer inside the centre crop and check it at thumbnail size. Use no poster frames or line drawings. Add a short, legible mono detail only if useful; a sample or dramatisation label is mandatory where relevant. Start video with the action or a human line, not a title card. Use moving type or a 23° cut sparingly and subtitle speech.
- Use the [dark primary lockup](assets/logo-dark.svg) on dark or the ink [light primary lockup](assets/logo-light.svg) on off-white. Keep at least 0.4em clear space, the bold caps tagline and no rule. The mark stays still. Use the [app icon](assets/app-icon.svg) via the [avatar artwork](assets/profile-picture.png). Keep the hairline and mono web versions as references only. Use the [phone-first cover guide](assets/phone-first-cover.svg) as a layout guide, not as publishable art.

### Cast and batch before launch

1. Run a private, consent-first casting call for adult players, coaches and volunteers. Ask the founder to record two direct-to-camera intros, one each for TikTok and LinkedIn. Log the founder's own approval too. Confirm locations, background faces and rights, and collect signed releases before filming for publication. Plan hands-only, unbranded dramatisations and sample figures as backups.
2. Start with **5 to 7 TikToks, 4 to 5 Instagram Reels plus Stories daily, and 3 LinkedIn posts weekly**: 2 from the founder's personal profile and 1 from the Company Page. Plan **10 to 15 distinct cuts** per shoot, including short openings and follow-ups. The [first-week schedule](account-setup.md) puts those slots on the calendar. A useful reply can take a TikTok slot.
3. Capture a recurring host, real action, natural sound and at least two usable stills. Cut a quick TikTok with a direct question, a Reel built for sharing with a photo cover, and a founder clip or silent LinkedIn receipt document. Export without watermarks or library music. Add app-specific captions and audio, and confirm approvals before anything is scheduled.

### Clubs and creators from week one

Invite **2 to 3 clubs** to agree in writing to one test story and one Instagram Collab post each, and shortlist **3 to 5 local sports creators** in Australia and Aotearoa New Zealand for paid commissions from week one, subject to separate approval before any fee or commitment. Look for evidence of local ties and useful replies, not just follower totals. Agree in writing on deliverables, fee if approved, deadlines, usage window, edits, tags, disclosure, removal and separate paid rights. Clubs and creators approve their involvement and any Collab invite; a club's approval does not replace individual releases. Use plain clothing and original material, with no team marks. If permissions or budget are not in place, hold the collaboration and run a founder or anonymous fallback.

### Phase 0 paid hook test: plan only

**No money is spent and no ad account is touched without the user's separate go-ahead.** Do not connect accounts, schedule ads, accept creator authorisations or enter payment details. After approval, propose an **A$50/day total ceiling for three days (A$150 maximum)**. Split the budget evenly between two versions of a cleared TikTok post using Spark Ads only if the creator separately agrees to paid use and disclosure. Change only the opening three seconds, keep the rest of the cut and audience the same, and compare three-second hold or the closest disclosed measure. Don't run a broad reach boost. Agree the audience and a minimum usable sample before spending; call small or uneven results inconclusive. Stop at the cap, at any rights concern, or if the test cannot answer the question. Never count paid results as organic.

## Growth targets and decision points

**Planning assumption, not a forecast or deadline:** 500,000 combined account follows over **24 to 36 months** from the first live week: **TikTok 300,000, Instagram 170,000, LinkedIn 30,000** (Company Page). Report founder reach separately. Counts across accounts aren't unique people. Use the time windows as review points, not permission to skip a gate. Revisit channel targets and assumptions every quarter. No bought followers, follow trades or empty giveaways.

| Phase and planning window | Main work | Measures and gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0 to 1,000: months 0 to 3** | Cast and clear adults, recruit club and creator partners, run the Postcode Call-Out, reply within one working day and prepare the paid hook test. | Track net follows, hold, full watches, shares and usable private nominations. Reach 1,000 and complete four weekly reviews; find two formats above their account median for shares or full watches twice each. |
| **1,000 to 10,000: months 3 to 9** | Repeat strong local stories with clubs and creators. Invite supporter submissions with written permission and feature only cleared athletes. | Track returning viewers, shares and saves per 1,000 views, usable nominations and cleared collaborations. Reach 10,000, complete two collaborations and see usable nominations from two verified postcodes for four consecutive weeks. |
| **10,000 to 100,000: months 9 to 18** | Repeat a proven series in both countries. If separately approved, repeat paid tests only where rights cover the use and comparing results is worthwhile. | Track returning viewers, nominations and serious sponsor enquiries by region; report cost per usable action separately if paid. Reach 100,000 and complete two successful publishing cycles in both countries; don't raise spend without approval and acceptable costs. |
| **100,000 to 500,000: months 18 to 36** | Expand cleared local creator stories and reserve staff time for moderation, corrections and rights checks. | Track net follows by account, repeat viewing, shares, nominations, enquiries and unresolved issues. At 500,000, keep the cadence only if four weeks of reviews show prompt replies, complete rights records and no open rights issues. |

## Evidence, rights and replies

- **Show the evidence:** Start with [AusPlay](https://www.ausport.gov.au/clearinghouse/research/ausplay), [Sport NZ Active NZ](https://sportnz.org.nz/research-and-insights/active-nz/) or a club fee schedule shared with permission. Record the source link or permission, publication year, location, who and what was measured, and the currency. Match each claim to its evidence: participation data doesn't by itself establish costs or a funding gap. Link the source in the post or on the last slide. If the figure isn't verified and cleared, omit it. Mark the A$2,500 split as a worked example, never a payment or promise.
- **Get written permission:** Have a signed release for anyone identifiable and rights for each photo, clip and sound. Record coverage of platforms, edits, duration, tagging and paid use. A roster flag alone is not clearance; never name example athletes or scouted prospects or publish an unverified athlete postcode. Get approval before publishing, tagging, sharing or boosting. No team logos, club emblems or trademarked jerseys.
- **Protect nominations:** Ask for the nominator's postcode in a private message, not identifying details. Ask them to speak to the adult first. For juniors, use a parent or guardian route only, never a direct conversation with a junior. Keep nominations private and hide comments that reveal personal information. Use the [reply templates, escalation path and moderation roster](account-setup.md#moderation-before-launch).
- **Music and tags:** Start with original sound. For Instagram and TikTok, select instrumental tracks in each app's commercial-use library, checking paid eligibility separately. Brief mood, tempo and search terms, not a named track, and don't move library audio between apps. Keep LinkedIn documents silent; use original or LinkedIn-cleared audio for video. Use a few relevant hashtags such as `#TMRWLine` and `#GrassrootsSport`. Tag only specifically cleared collaborators or subjects who approved the tag. Never tag benchmarks. Repost only with written permission from the rights holder and those shown.

### Review every Monday

Record the opening and **three-second hold as the first measure for every video**: viewers reaching three seconds divided by starts when available. If a platform doesn't report it, label the nearest available retention measure and don't compare unlike measures across platforms. Then record full watches, shares and saves per 1,000 views, repeat viewing, net follows and reply time. Count usable nominations once when the postcode is in scope and there is a permission-safe route back. Count an enquiry when a business names an area and asks for a next step. Keep private details out of the review and paid separate from unpaid.

Pick one thing to repeat, one opening to test and one cut to pause. After four weeks, keep a series if it beats its own account median for shares or retention twice or consistently brings usable nominations or enquiries. If a series misses all three, pause it and test one replacement, not three. Check the current gate, rights and moderation coverage. Give each decision an owner and a date.
